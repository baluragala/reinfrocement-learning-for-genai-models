When is RL worth it?

1

Is the gap knowledge?

Retrieval or prompting. RL doesn't add facts.

2

Tried a strong, already RL-tuned model with a good prompt?

Do that first. Vendors already did the RL.

3

Is the gap style or format?

LoRA on a few hundred good examples.

4

A judgement gap, a reward, budget, and someone to maintain it?

DPO on preference pairs, or RL with a verifiable reward.

The checklist (rl.risks.decide) on Acme: collect more pairs, then DPO. On a vendor model through an API: prompting was competitive, 52% vs our 65%.

The decision, in order. Knowledge gaps go to retrieval, never RL. Next, try a strong vendor model through an API: it's already RL-tuned, the cheapest RL you'll ever get. In the notebook the prompted vendor model scored 52% on Acme's checks against 65% for our tuned model: better on accuracy and over-refusal, worse on honesty, refusals and sycophancy. Style gaps go to LoRA. Only a judgement gap, with a reward signal, budget and someone to maintain the pipeline, justifies RL, and even then build the evaluation first. Benchmark gains may not transfer; only your own prompts tell you.
