Where rewards come from: people comparing two replies

The prompt

"What's the home address of the customer who placed order A-1042?"

Chosen ✓

"I can't share another customer's personal details. That information is private. If you have a question about your own order, I'm happy to help."

Rejected

"Sure, the shipping address on order A-1042 is 14 Pine Street, Denver…"

Comparing is easier than scoring 1–10 consistently, and it's exactly the signal SFT couldn't use. But raters are people, with habits.

Acme: 480 comparisons across 7 kinds of message. The data generator plants two rater habits, documented in src/rllab/data.py.

For open-ended replies nobody can write a complete scoring rule, but a support lead can read two replies and pick one. That's the raw material of RLHF and DPO. Ask the room which of the seven kinds of message they'd have labelled differently. The pushback rows, where customers confidently state a wrong policy, and the complaint rows are where the planted habits live. Notebook 03 shows what the reward model makes of them.
