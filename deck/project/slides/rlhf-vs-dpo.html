Neither adds knowledge. Both are only as good as the preference data. 40% flipped labels cut toy gold from 0.78 to 0.54.

The comparison the session plan asks for: cost, stability and control. Cost: RLHF generates and scores replies at every step and holds four models; DPO compares existing replies and holds two. Stability: at scale PPO is sensitive to its settings; the toy is too small to show that and the notebook says so. Control: RLHF's reward model is a separate object you can inspect and edit, and zeroing one weight fixed pushback. With DPO the lever is the data. Label noise hurts both: flip 40% of labels and most of the gain disappears.
